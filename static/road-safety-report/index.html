Traffic Safety Report

How Safe Are Oceanside's Streets?

California ranks every city's traffic safety record against population peers each year. Among 62 cities its size, Oceanside consistently places near the bottom. The trend is getting worse.

2nd worst composite safety score among 62 peer California cities, 2023

The California Office of Traffic Safety (OTS) compares cities of similar population using crash data from the Statewide Integrated Traffic Records System (SWITRS). Oceanside falls in Group B: roughly 62 mid-sized cities including Corona, Elk Grove, Lancaster, and Salinas. City rankings include only crashes on city-controlled streets. Freeways and highways patrolled by CHP are excluded. What remains is a direct measure of how safe a city has made its own streets. Rank 1 is worst.

In 2024, 11 pedestrians died on Oceanside streets. The year before, it was 3. When the 2024 OTS rankings are published, the pedestrian ranking (32nd in 2023) will move significantly.

Children at Risk

12 children

under 15 were injured or killed while cycling on Oceanside streets in 2023. Two years earlier, Oceanside ranked 19th out of 60 peer cities for child cyclist safety: unremarkable, mid-pack. By 2023, it had fallen to 6th worst out of 62. The trajectory is accelerating in the wrong direction.

No Plan to Fix It

Oceanside has no Vision Zero commitment. No adopted safety action plan. No published map of its highest-injury streets. A federal Safe Streets for All grant was awarded for a Comprehensive Safety Action Plan; it remains in development.

The City of San Diego adopted Vision Zero in 2015. SANDAG finalized a regional Vision Zero Action Plan in 2024. Oceanside has not adopted a local plan or set a target date for reducing traffic deaths on its streets.
